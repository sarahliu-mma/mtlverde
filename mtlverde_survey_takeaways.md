# MTLVerde — Website Feedback: Main Takeaways

**n = 13 respondents · 100% used the English site · 69.2% on mobile**

---

## Who responded

- **38.5%** (5/13) identify as new to the city; 30.8% have lived in Montréal under 6 months.
- **92.3%** (12/13) had attended a Montréal community event or festival before — an experienced, already-motivated audience.
- Respondents cluster in central boroughs: Downtown, Ville-Marie, the Plateau, Nuns Island, Old Port.
- Every respondent used the English version, and roughly seven in ten browsed on a phone.

## The main task works

- **Finding a free weekend event was easy:** 61.5% rated it 5/5, 76.9% rated 4–5, and **nobody scored below 3**.
- **69.2%** (9/13) noticed and used the borough/category filters.
- **76.9%** say they're likely to use the site for real event discovery (4–5 out of 5); the rest sat at 3, with no low scores.

## Aggregation and language are real draws for respondants

The open-ended "why would you use this" answers converge on two themes that aren't captured in any single metric:

- **One place instead of many.** Respondents describe event info as scattered across social media and individual venue sites — "too many things thrown at us on socials." Consolidation is the value.
- **English access to French-only listings.** One respondent noted that events "on their actual websites are only available in french, but this brings it together and offers it in english." For a newcomer audience this is a real barrier the site removes.

## Sustainability is important to respondents, but is still a secondary motivator

- **61.5%** rate sustainability as important (4–5) when choosing an event — while 2 respondents rated it a 1, so interest is real but not universal.
- **76.9%** found the eco-badge scoring (transit / walk-in / outdoor) self-explanatory (4–5); **84.6%** found the sustainability page easy to navigate.
- Comments call the scoring "a logical way" to rate events and "a super user friendly resource especially for a newcomer."
- One respondent framed the hierarchy directly: they'd use the site mainly to find events, "not really prioritizing the sustainability rating, although it's nice to have that info as well." Sustainability enriches the experience; discovery drives the visit.

## The two AI features perform similiarly

- **Adoption is about half for each:** 53.8% used the Chatbot, 53.8% used Ask MTLVerde.
- **Relevance is also matched:** among those who used them, 60% said each tool returned relevant events.
- **Preference splits with no winner:** 38.5% Both, 38.5% Ask MTLVerde, 15.4% Chatbot, 7.7% Neither.
- Free-text responses suggest users perceive them as one thing — "Is the chatbot different from the MTLVerde assistant?" and "they pretty much do the same thing."
- When an answer misses, **53.8%** would rephrase the question and **30.8%** would give up.
- Non-festival and general public-event queries are mostly untested: 61.5% haven't tried them.

## Main Takeaways

Structured navigation outperformed conversational navigation. The filters were noticed and used by 69.2%, and the guided find-an-event task scored near the top of the scale — while the AI tools reached only about half of respondents. The site's clearest strength is being a single, English-accessible, filterable index of Montréal community events, with sustainability scoring as a well-received layer on top.
